What are show notes?

Show notes are the written companion to each podcast episode. They typically include a short episode summary, chapter timestamps, key takeaways, and links to resources mentioned on the show. Good show notes help with SEO, make your episode accessible to deaf and hard-of-hearing listeners, and give your audience a way to skip to the parts they care about.

Anatomy of great show notes

  • Episode title + number — clear and scannable
  • One-sentence hook — what the episode is actually about
  • Chapter timestamps — 5–10 per hour of audio
  • Key takeaways — 3–5 bullet points
  • Guest bio + links — if you have a guest
  • Resources mentioned — books, tools, links
  • Social media snippets — 2–3 quotable lines
  • Call to action — subscribe, review, share

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an AI API to use this?

No. This generator uses pattern matching and text analysis entirely in your browser. It extracts timestamps, identifies topic transitions, and structures the output — no data is sent anywhere.

How many chapters should my show notes have?

A good rule is 1 chapter per 5–8 minutes of audio. For a 45-minute episode, aim for 6–9 chapters. Too few and the notes aren't useful; too many and listeners can't scan them quickly.

What's the ideal length of show notes?

For most podcasts, 200–500 words is ideal. This includes the summary, chapter list, and key takeaways. Long-form interview shows can go up to 800 words. Anything over 1,000 words usually means you're transcribing rather than summarizing.

Do show notes help with SEO?

Yes. Show notes are indexed by Google and can drive search traffic to your episode page. Include your episode's main keywords in the title, summary, and chapter headings. Podcast directories like Apple Podcasts and Spotify also index show notes for in-app search.

Should I include timestamps even without chapters?

Yes, if your podcast app supports chapters (Apple Podcasts and most modern apps do). Timestamps let listeners jump directly to sections. Even without app support, showing timestamps in the episode description gives listeners a way to manually skip.

Note: This tool produces a structured draft based on pattern matching. It does not understand the content of your episode. Always review, edit, and add context before publishing.
